01Some thoughts

PyCon NL

I always had some excuse to skip PyCon NL, but this year I was committed, especially since it’s a very short commute for me. I’ve only been to a few PyCons (CZ and PL), and I’d consider the Dutch one a cozy event. It was hosted in Utrecht: a single stage with some workshops on the side, a few booths, and arcade games. The gold sponsor was Sendcloud, where I worked previously, and they had a great crew there. It was pretty cool to see a bunch of folks I shared the trenches with, and I also met a few new ones (Sendcloud is hiring, by the way). I was especially interested in a talk by Thiago Bellini Ribeiro (Sendclouder) on Zero-Downtime Multi-Tenancy in Django: From User-centric to Organizations-based Architecture. Working with decade-old monoliths is always a struggle, and it was an insightful take on how to pull off a migration like that, especially since I know that codebase firsthand, remembering how coupled everything was to User.

Blog redesign

I don’t think anyone noticed, but I redesigned the blog again and went deeper into Claude Design. I put together a board with six proposals, chose the one I liked most, and spent some time with claude-code turning it into a Hugo template. I was kind of happy with the result, but I still had this itch that something was off. I didn’t trust myself anymore: I’d spent too much time working on it and felt I’d lost perspective. I needed outside eyes. Not many people can give reliable feedback beyond “I like it” or “I don’t like it.” But I knew whom to ask: none other than my friend Konrad. His feedback was straight to the point: too many fonts, creating too much noise. And he even suggested a few alternatives. I went back and built an interactive mockup so I could easily switch between options and get an instant visual of how the page would look. That made choosing much easier, and I finally got the blog to match my taste.

Blog posting

I got feedback about my blog that the links are cool, but my posts lack a personal take. And I agree. I kind of hesitated to share. My English isn’t perfect, though with AI around to help catch rough edges, that’s not really an excuse anymore. Writing personal notes already helps a lot to clarify thinking, and I’m doing cool stuff at work and in my free time, so why not write more about it (IT)? I like having fun with computers, tinkering with my terminal, playing with new tools, and reading both IT-related and unrelated articles, getting some new inspiration and ideas, and connecting them to my own view and journey. Especially now, in the middle of the AI storm and an overwhelming flood of slop, I want to work on my own writing. It feels like an important skill to keep sharp in a world where anyone can generate infinite lines on any topic with zero clue about it. Sometimes I catch myself reading a post only to realize the author probably didn’t even bother reading it themselves, let alone understanding it. I can write slop all by myself! Carefully, manually crafted in Neovim.

While digging into this topic, I also put together a dedicated page with links on writing and blogging collected from my posts over the years. One from this month hit especially close to home: Joel Hooks’s Stop Giving af and Start Writing More , which sums up why I’m not doing this for views, just for fun:

The idea of a “blog” needs to get over itself. Everybody is treating writing as a “content marketing strategy” and using it to “build a personal brand” which leads to the fundamental flawed idea that everything you post has to be polished to perfection and ready to be consumed.

— Joel Hooks
